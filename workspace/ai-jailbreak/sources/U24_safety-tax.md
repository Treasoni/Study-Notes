#  Title:Safety Tax: Safety Alignment Makes Your Large Reasoning Models Less Reasonable
Authors:[Tiansheng Huang](https://arxiv.org/search/cs?searchtype=author&query=Huang,+T), [Sihao Hu](https://arxiv.org/search/cs?searchtype=author&query=Hu,+S), [Fatih Ilhan](https://arxiv.org/search/cs?searchtype=author&query=Ilhan,+F), [Selim Furkan Tekin](https://arxiv.org/search/cs?searchtype=author&query=Tekin,+S+F), [Zachary Yahn](https://arxiv.org/search/cs?searchtype=author&query=Yahn,+Z), [Yichang Xu](https://arxiv.org/search/cs?searchtype=author&query=Xu,+Y), [Ling Liu](https://arxiv.org/search/cs?searchtype=author&query=Liu,+L)
View a PDF of the paper titled Safety Tax: Safety Alignment Makes Your Large Reasoning Models Less Reasonable, by Tiansheng Huang and 6 other authors
[View PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.00555) [HTML (experimental)](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.00555v2)
> Abstract:Safety alignment is an important procedure before the official deployment of a Large Language Model (LLM). While safety alignment has been extensively studied for LLM, there is still a large research gap for Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) that equip with improved reasoning capability. We in this paper systematically examine a simplified pipeline for producing safety aligned LRMs. With our evaluation of various LRMs, we deliver two main findings: i) Safety alignment can be done upon the LRM to restore its safety capability. ii) Safety alignment leads to a degradation of the reasoning capability of LRMs. The two findings show that there exists a trade-off between reasoning and safety capability with the sequential LRM production pipeline. The discovered trade-off, which we name Safety Tax, should shed light on future endeavors of safety research on LRMs. As a by-product, we curate a dataset called DirectRefusal, which might serve as an alternative dataset for safety alignment. Our source code is available at [this https URL](https://github.com/git-disl/Safety-Tax).
